A town hall shouldn’t leave people guessing.

Company-wide communication has a big audience and a small window of attention.

Working with Microsoft through a partner, I created town hall toolkits and templates to support clearer company-wide communications. The focus was straightforward: help the people preparing the communication organise what mattered—and help employees understand it.

Because bringing everyone together is only useful if the message comes together, too.

The challenge: one event, plenty to explain

A town hall needs to do more than fit updates into an agenda.

It needs a clear thread: what matters, why it matters and what employees should take away. Without that structure, even useful information can become a succession of announcements competing for attention.

The communication task was to create a framework for those messages without making every contribution sound like it had come out of the same corporate biscuit cutter.

Consistency, yes. Identical sentences, no.

What I made

Town hall toolkits · Templates for company-wide communications

The materials provided a reusable foundation for preparing internal communications, with an emphasis on clarity, structure and employee relevance.

The portfolio examples are illustrative recreations. Original materials are confidential.

My approach: give the message a backbone

Put the point before the preamble

I focused on helping the communication establish its purpose early.

What did employees need to know? Why was it worth their attention? Those questions gave the content a centre of gravity.

The opening shouldn’t need its own executive summary.

Create structure without scripting every word

The templates gave contributors a framework for organising information while leaving room for the subject and speaker.

The aim was a recognisable communication structure—not a recognisable case of template fatigue.

Keep the employee’s question in view

Company-wide updates can explain what the organisation is doing and still leave employees wondering what it means for them.

I brought that reader’s perspective into the work: make the context useful, the main message visible and any next step clear.

Make the framework reusable

The toolkit format supported a repeatable approach rather than treating every communication as a blank-page exercise.

The point wasn’t to remove judgement. It was to give that judgement a useful starting place.

My contribution

Working through a partner, I:

  • Created town hall toolkits and company-wide communication templates.
  • Helped structure messages around their purpose and employee relevance.
  • Connected editorial clarity with the practical needs of communication preparation.

What this case demonstrates

This case focuses on the toolkit work—not a claim that templates alone transformed employee engagement.

It shows how I translate communication needs into practical editorial resources: enough structure to guide preparation, enough flexibility to accommodate different messages and a consistent focus on the audience.

The broader internal communication programme has its own reported results. Those should remain separate unless the toolkit’s specific contribution can be established.

The takeaway

A good toolkit doesn’t do the thinking for people.

It helps them spend less time finding a structure and more time deciding what is worth saying.

The goal: company-wide communication with a clear point—not just a large invitation list.
