AI, beyond the announcement

“AI-first” looks good on a strategy slide. Then someone has to explain it.

2: AI Newsletter and monthly AI Digest
Focus
Making AI-first strategy understandable

For the company’s Vision 2024 & Beyond programme, I created an AI Newsletter and a monthly AI Digest—turning AI-first ambition into clear stories and practical learning content.

Because hearing about AI and understanding it are two different things.

The challenge: fewer buzzwords, more lightbulbs

The programme set the direction. Employees needed a way into the subject.

My task was to make AI approachable without making it superficial: explain the ideas, connect them to the strategy and give people something worth reading.

“AI will change everything” was not going to fill two publications. Nor should it.

What I made

AI Newsletter · Monthly AI Digest

Two publications. One editorial brief: make AI easier to understand, not harder to escape.

What I did

Gave the strategy a storyline

I turned the programme’s AI-first direction into clear editorial stories.

The ambition stayed. The language had to earn its place.

Put learning before fanfare

I created practical learning content that helped explain AI rather than simply announce its importance.

Less “the future is here.” More “here’s what this means.”

Took it beyond launch day

The Newsletter and monthly Digest gave AI a recurring place in the company’s communication.

A strategy needs a life after the presentation. These publications gave people a reason to return to the subject.

The results

The work recorded a 35% improvement in digital engagement and launched two AI publications.

That is an engagement result—not proof that everyone became an AI expert between meetings. Keeping the claim precise makes it stronger.

The takeaway

The company had an AI ambition. I gave it something people could read, learn from and come back to.

Because “AI-first” should be the start of an explanation. Not the whole thing.
