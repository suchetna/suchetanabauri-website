Services

Branding

Shaping how your brand looks, sounds, and shows up.

Discuss your brand

Your brand is more than how it speaks. It is how people recognise you, understand what you offer, and experience your business.

I help bring those parts together—from strategy and identity to the details people encounter every day.

So your website, presentation, and customer experience feel like the same organisation. Ideally, the one you intended.

Approach

A clear idea. A connected identity.

A distinctive logo and a well-written tagline need something solid behind them: a clear understanding of who you are, who you serve, and why someone should choose you.

I work with you to establish that foundation and translate it into a brand people can recognise and your team can use.

The focus is coherence: making the different parts work together, without making everything look and sound identical. Consistency is useful. Cloning is a different service.

What I do

From what you stand for to how people experience it.

Brand Strategy

Know what you want to be known for.

Clarify your positioning, your audience, and the value you bring. Define what makes your business relevant—and what gives people a reason to choose it.

The result is a foundation for decisions about your identity, messaging, and experience. Not a collection of ambitions that could belong to any business with a meeting room.

Brand Architecture

Give everything a clear place.

Make the relationships between your business, brands, services, and products easier to understand.

I help organise how your offers are named, grouped, and connected, so people can see what belongs where—and your next service does not require an explanatory diagram and a patient colleague.

Verbal Identity

Sound like yourself. Across the whole business.

Develop the language that expresses your brand: its name, core messages, voice, tone, and key phrases.

Then put it to work across websites, presentations, campaigns, and everyday communication. A customer email and a launch announcement should not sound identical. They should, however, appear to know each other.

Visual Identity

Make the brand recognisable before anyone reads a word.

Bring your strategy into a visual identity through logo design, typography, colour, imagery, and graphic elements.

The aim is a connected visual system that works across formats—not just a logo that looks excellent in the middle of an otherwise empty slide.

Brand Guidelines

Make the right choices easier to repeat.

Bring your verbal and visual identity into practical guidance for the people using it.

Set out clear principles, examples, and applications that help teams and creative partners work consistently. Useful enough to open while working. Not merely impressive enough to approve.

Brand Experiences

Carry the idea through to the encounter.

Connect your identity to the places where people meet your business—from your website and social channels to presentations, onboarding materials, and customer communication.

The aim is to make the experience reflect the promise. “Easy to work with” should survive the enquiry form.

Process

How we work together.

  1. 01

    Understand the business

    Explore your goals, audience, offers, and existing identity. Identify what is working, what is unclear, and what no longer reflects the business you are building.

  2. 02

    Set the direction

    Agree on the positioning, brand structure, and central idea. Establish a clear basis for creative decisions before debating which shade of blue feels more confident.

  3. 03

    Develop the identity

    Shape the verbal and visual elements around the agreed direction. Explore how the brand communicates, what makes it recognisable, and how its parts work together.

  4. 04

    Test it in context

    Apply the identity to selected materials and touchpoints. Check whether it works in the places you need it—not just in the presentation introducing it.

  5. 05

    Prepare it for everyday use

    Bring the assets, guidance, and templates together. Help your team understand how to use them, what can flex, and what needs to stay consistent.

Scope

What this can include.

The scope can bring together:

  • Brand research and existing-brand reviews
  • Positioning and value propositions
  • Brand, product, and service architecture
  • Naming and naming principles
  • Brand narratives and core messaging
  • Voice and tone development
  • Logos and visual identity systems
  • Typography, colour, imagery, and graphic direction
  • Verbal and visual brand guidelines
  • Website, presentation, and communication templates
  • Brand applications across key touchpoints
  • Rollout materials and team guidance

Build a brand that holds together.

Whether you are starting something new, bringing several offers under one name, or updating an identity you have outgrown, the goal is the same: make your business easier to understand, recognise, and choose.

And make sure the next person creating a slide does not have to reinvent it.
